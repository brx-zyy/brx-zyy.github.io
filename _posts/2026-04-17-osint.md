# OSINT Methodology & Tools

**OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence)** is the process of collecting and analyzing publicly available information to discover useful insights about a target.

## Overview

OSINT works best as a repeatable process rather than a single search.

1. Establish a collect, verify, document, report loop instead of treating OSINT as a one off lookup.
2. Master search engine operators and dork combinations to surface information generic searches miss entirely.
3. Pivot from a single data point, a username, email address, or phone number, into a full identity map using dedicated enumeration tools.
4. Pull further leads out of images, embedded metadata, and visual geolocation clues.
5. Cross-reference social media activity, public records, and domain infrastructure to corroborate or refute earlier findings.
6. Reach for advanced techniques, forensic metadata, satellite imagery, breach corpora, social graphing, and stylometry.

---

## 1. The OSINT Process

A repeatable loop, not a one-off search:

1. **Collect.** Gather raw data points from a seed: name, email, handle, image, phone, domain.
2. **Pivot.** Use each new data point to open a new search vector. A discovered username gets searched everywhere else.
3. **Verify.** Corroborate any claim with two or more independent sources before treating it as fact.
4. **Document.** Timestamp, screenshot, and cite the source URL for every finding.
5. **Report.** Organize findings by category (identity, location, employment, associates, digital footprint, infrastructure) rather than as a narrative dump.

## 2. Search Engine Operators

| Operator | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `site:` | Restrict to a domain | `site:linkedin.com "brxzyy"` |
| `filetype:` | Restrict to a file type | `"brxzyy" filetype:pdf OR filetype:xlsx` |
| `intitle:` | Term in page title | `intitle:"resume" "brxzyy"` |
| `inurl:` | Term in URL | `inurl:profile "brxzyy"` |
| `intext:` | Term in body text | `intext:"brxzyy.me@"` |
| `-word` | Exclude a term | `"brxzyy" -site:facebook.com` |
| `" "` | Exact phrase | `"123 Main St"` |
| `AROUND(n)` | Proximity search | `"brxzyy" AROUND(3) "Acme Corp"` |
| `cache:` | Google's cached copy | `cache:example.com/page` |

**Useful combinations**
- `site:pastebin.com "brxzyy@email.com"` checks for leaked-data mentions.
- `site:*.gov " brxzyy "` surfaces official/government records.
- `"brxzyy" (resume OR CV) filetype:pdf` finds self-published resumes, which often leak phone numbers and employment history.
- `site:targetcompany.com filetype:pptx OR filetype:xlsx` finds internal decks or spreadsheets left publicly indexed.

**Reference collections**
- Google Hacking Database (GHDB): curated dork strings, hosted on Exploit-DB.
- Bishop Fox OSINT dork cheat sheets.
- IntelTechniques Search Tools (Michael Bazzell): prebuilt search links across dozens of platforms.
- ExploitDB Google Dorks list and OSINT Dojo's dork compilations for niche use cases.

## 3. Username, Email & Phone Pivoting

**Username enumeration**
- **Sherlock**: CLI, checks a username across 400+ sites in one pass.
- **Maigret**: similar to Sherlock, but pulls richer profile metadata (bio, avatar, linked accounts) and exports a full report.
- **WhatsMyName**: community-maintained site list, strong for niche/regional platforms Sherlock misses.
- **Namechk**: quick web-based check across social platforms and domain registrars.
- **Blackbird**: newer OSINT username checker focused on speed and structured export.
- **Userrecon / Whatsmyname CLI forks**: lightweight alternatives for offline or scripted runs.
- **KnowEm**: web tool checking username and brand availability across hundreds of networks.

**Email pivoting**
- **Have I Been Pwned (HIBP)**: confirms whether an email appears in known breach dumps and lists which services it was tied to.
- **Holehe**: CLI tool checking which platforms an email is registered on via password-reset/signup flow probes, no login attempted.
- **Epieos**: reverse email lookup; surfaces linked Google account artifacts (Maps reviews, calendar name, profile photo).
- **Hunter.io**: corporate email-pattern discovery (`first.last@company.com` conventions) for organization recon.
- **GHunt**: deep-dives a Gmail address for linked Google services metadata.
- **Mosint**: automated email OSINT tool combining breach checks, social presence, and metadata in one run.
- **EmailRep.io**: reputation and risk scoring for an email address based on aggregated signals.

**Phone number pivoting**
- **Truecaller, Sync.me**: carrier/caller-ID lookups.
- **That'sThem, Whitepages**: US-focused people-search aggregators (name/address/phone correlation).
- **PhoneInfoga**: CLI tool for footprinting a phone number, carrier lookup, and format validation.
- **NumLookup, Spokeo**: additional reverse phone lookup aggregators for cross-checking results.
- Reverse-search a number in quotes on Google; marketplace listings and forum signatures often surface it.

## 4. Image & Facial OSINT

- **Reverse image search**: Google Images, Yandex Images (notably stronger for faces and Eastern European sources), TinEye (best for finding the earliest known instance of an image, useful for debunking), Bing Visual Search.
- **PimEyes**: facial-recognition reverse search across the indexed web. Treat hits as leads to verify, not confirmed matches.
- **FaceCheck.ID**: alternative facial search engine, useful as a cross-check against PimEyes results.
- **ExifTool**: pulls metadata (`exiftool image.jpg`) from an original, uncompressed image file: GPS coordinates, device model, timestamp. Most platforms strip EXIF on upload, so this mainly matters for directly shared files.
- **Jeffrey's Exif Viewer**: browser-based alternative for a quick metadata check without installing anything.
- **Geolocation from visual clues**: cross-reference signage language/alphabet, license plate format, driving side, architecture, vegetation, and shadow angle against Google Street View or Mapillary.
- **SunCalc**: estimates time of day and date range from shadow length and sun angle in a photo.
- **Forensically / FotoForensics**: error-level analysis and other tools for spotting image edits or composites.
- **GeoSpy / Overpass Turbo**: newer AI-assisted and map-query tools for narrowing down a photo's likely location.

## 5. Social Media & People-Search Aggregators

- **Social Searcher**: real-time cross-platform mention search by name or keyword.
- **Platform-native pivots** that work even when a friends/followers list is private:
  - Public tagged photos, check-ins, and comments or likes on public posts frequently leak associate names.
  - LinkedIn: current/past employers, skill endorsements, posts liked.
  - Instagram/Facebook "photos of" tab and per-post geotags, often more reliable than a stated bio location.
- **IntelTechniques.com** (Bazzell): the most complete free hub of prebuilt lookup tools, organized by data type.
- **OSINT Framework** (osintframework.com): categorized tree of tools by data type; the standard starting map for any investigation.
- **Social-Analyzer**: automated tool that checks a name/username across a large set of social platforms and scores likely matches.
- **Twint / snscrape alternatives**: for scraping public X/Twitter data where API access is limited (check current ToS before use).
- **Sowl / Osintgram**: Instagram-focused OSINT scraping utilities for public profile data.

## 6. Public Records & Document Sources

- **Court records**: PACER (US federal), state court portals, UniCourt (aggregator), CourtListener (free federal case search).
- **Property/land records**: county assessor sites, usually searchable by owner name.
- **Business filings**: OpenCorporates, state Secretary of State registries, useful for finding registered LLCs, co-founders, and registered-agent addresses.
- **Academic footprint**: Google Scholar, ResearchGate, ORCID, university faculty/staff directories.
- **Archived/deleted content**: Wayback Machine (web.archive.org), archive.today, cachedview.nl, critical for recovering a deleted bio, old job title, or removed post.
- **Government/regulatory registries**: SAM.gov (US federal contractor registry), USAspending.gov, and equivalent national procurement portals for organizational recon.

## 7. Domain & Infrastructure Recon

- **whois**: registrant info, often redacted today, but registrar and creation date remain useful.
- **crt.sh**: certificate transparency logs; enumerates subdomains that have ever had a TLS certificate issued.
- **Shodan / Censys**: internet-wide scan data for exposed services and devices tied to an org's IP ranges. Only query results, never connect to discovered services without authorization.
- **theHarvester**: aggregates emails, subdomains, and names tied to a domain from search engines and public sources.
- **Amass / Subfinder / Assetfinder**: active and passive subdomain enumeration for a wider attack-surface map.
- **BuiltWith / Wappalyzer**: tech-stack fingerprinting from public-facing sites.
- **DNSDumpster**: free visual DNS recon and subdomain mapping.
- **SecurityTrails**: historical DNS and passive DNS data, useful for tracking infrastructure changes over time.
- **FOFA / ZoomEye**: Shodan-equivalent scan engines with stronger coverage in some regions.

## 8. Advanced Techniques

### 8.1 Metadata forensics beyond EXIF
- `exiftool file.pdf` or `pdfinfo` on PDFs/Office files often reveals the author's real name, internal username, software version, and creation/edit timestamps, even after visible identifying text has been removed. Corporate templates frequently retain the name of whoever originally built them.
- Google Docs/Office 365 files shared "anyone with the link" sometimes expose version history with prior edits and other editors' names.
- Unusual embedded fonts in a PDF can hint at the software or region of origin.
- **FOCA** (Fingerprinting Organizations with Collected Archives): automates bulk metadata extraction from an organization's publicly indexed documents.

### 8.2 Satellite & aerial correlation
- **Google Earth Pro (desktop)** has a historical-imagery slider that web Google Maps lacks, letting you roll back years of satellite passes to date when a structure appeared.
- **Sentinel Hub / Copernicus Browser**: free access to Sentinel-2 imagery when Google's coverage is outdated for a region.
- **NASA Worldview**: near-daily global satellite imagery, useful for large-scale environmental or event correlation.
- Street View capture dates, visible in the UI, can help date a background object precisely.

### 8.3 Wi-Fi / BSSID geolocation
- Wi-Fi access point MAC addresses (BSSIDs) are crowdsourced into geolocation databases as phones and laptops report seen networks. **WiGLE.net** lets you search a BSSID, obtained from a photo of a router or a screenshot showing nearby SSIDs, and get its approximate physical location.

### 8.4 Breach & leak corpus analysis
- Beyond HIBP, which only confirms an email was in a breach, gated tools like Dehashed or IntelligenceX let you search leaked-credential corpora for a specific email/username and surface associated usernames or secondary emails used at signup. Use only where explicitly authorized, and never to attempt access against a live account.
- Password pattern analysis, not literal reuse, is a legitimate correlation signal: mutated variants of a password across services in authorized investigations.

### 8.5 Social graph / network analysis
- Build the graph, not just the profile list: who likes whose posts, who's repeatedly tagged together, shared group memberships. **Maltego** (free CE transforms) or a manual **Gephi** graph turns disconnected profiles into a map showing high-connectivity "hub" people, often a family member with laxer privacy settings.
- Second-degree pivoting: a subject's own privacy may be locked down, but a sibling's old public album may not be.
- **SpiderFoot**: automates much of this graphing process by chaining dozens of data sources against a single seed.

### 8.6 Linguistic & stylometric analysis
- Comparing writing style (word choice, punctuation habits, characteristic typos, emoji use) across a known account and a suspected alt account is a real technique for linking identities when no other overlap exists. **JGAAP** formalizes this; manually, look for idiosyncratic repeated phrases.
- Timezone/posting-pattern analysis: plotting the hour-of-day distribution of posts can reveal a real timezone even when a stated location is spoofed.

### 8.7 Archive-diving beyond Wayback
- **Common Crawl**: free, bulk web archive dataset, useful when Wayback missed a historical snapshot.
- **archive.today**: manually archived pages a user or journalist saved before deletion.
- Deleted-post recovery tools, availability varies, can occasionally surface content still referenced in an account's history metadata.

### 8.8 Vehicle & object-based OSINT
- State/country-specific license plate design references help identify the issuing jurisdiction from a partial or blurred plate.
- Vehicle make/model/year identification from a partial photo (bumper, headlight shape) is its own sub-discipline in geolocation work.

### 8.9 Corporate/organizational recon
- **SEC EDGAR full-text search**: searches every US public company filing (10-K, proxy statements, Form 4s), which can name executives, stock holdings, and board relationships.
- Job postings (Indeed, LinkedIn) frequently leak internal tech stack, team structure, and internal tool names.
- Conference talks and GitHub commit history: engineers often reuse the same handle across platforms; commit timestamps can reveal likely working hours and timezone.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator / RocketReach**: for mapping org charts and role changes at scale during organizational recon.

## 9. Verification Discipline

- **Corroborate before recording.** A single matching-name profile isn't a match; confirm with a second independent detail (photo, employer, city, mutual connection).
- **Watch for stale data.** People-search aggregators often surface old addresses or jobs; date-stamp everything and prefer the most recent primary source.
- **Watch for decoy or abandoned profiles.** Cross-check at least two attributes before treating any single record as confirmed.
- **Screenshot and archive.** Use the Wayback Machine's "Save Page Now" or a timestamped local screenshot for anything that might be edited or deleted later.
